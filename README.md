# Kirjastot
A collection of library projects

Library Innovation & Community Expansion Repo
A collection of open-source frameworks, spatial designs, media experiments, and tooling concepts aimed at increasing community engagement and foot traffic.
Overview
This repository serves as a working backlog for initiatives designed to transform the local library into an active, multi-disciplinary community hub.
Project Backlog
1. Space & Layout Optimization
 * Interactive Floor Plan Model: A physical or modular layout featuring movable furniture pieces to allow staff and visitors to test different spatial configurations hands-on and observe traffic flow.
 * Space-Use Evaluation: Ongoing spatial mapping to identify dead zones and create inviting zones for community lingering, small events, or quiet study.
2. Local Heritage & Community Maps (QGIS Practice)
 * Local Discovery Map: A printed, pocket-sized neighborhood map highlighting points of interest, quiet walking routes, and local history notes.
 * Themed Walking Tours: Specialty maps—such as a Wivi Lönn tour—designed to guide visitors through specific local learning touchpoints.
3. Open-Source Digital Asset Library
 * Custom Iconography & Graphics: A unified collection of icons, templates, and visual assets for social media and flyers.
 * Licensing Model: Open-source architecture (e.g., Creative Commons) allowing local creators to contribute, ensuring the library owns its branding without recurring software or agency costs.
4. Spatial Media & 360 Production
 * Immersive 360 Skits & Content: Utilizing built-in library architecture (curtains and room dividers) to stage mini-productions, hide crew members, and test immersive storytelling formats.
 * Architecture-as-Set Design: Documenting and mapping the space's acoustic and visual layout (such as hidden kitchen and curtained alcoves) to create a repeatable framework for community media projects and live-stream experiments.
5. Augmented Reality & Spatial Access
 * AR Bookshelf Posters: Placing posters of bookshelves on doors or walls where specific book spines act as image-recognition targets (via QR or AR tracking).
 * Place-Based Digital Access: Exploring geofenced or localised access models where specific digital books or archival materials are only unlocked while physically present in or immediately around the library.
6. Micro-Maker Space & Tooling
 * Ergonomic & Safety Audits: Reviewing equipment placement (e.g., paper guillotines) to balance child safety constraints with adult accessibility.
 * Low-Footprint Production Tools: Curating compact, high-utility hardware optimised for small spaces, such as sticker printer-cutters and a button/pin press.
 * Enclosed Digital Fabrication: Designing safety-first enclosures for 3D printers or small laser cutters featuring integrated air filtration systems to safely handle fumes in a shared public space.
